Questions

Things you might
be wondering

I don't have trauma. Is this still for me?
+
Yes, and this question is exactly why this community exists. You don't need a diagnosis, a breakdown, or a story that qualifies. If something feels quietly off, if you're functioning but not quite in it, if nothing is technically wrong but something isn't yours, that's enough. The nervous system doesn't distinguish between "big" events and chronic low-grade pressure. Both leave patterns. You don't need to justify being here.
I've tried therapy, mindfulness, somatic work before. Will this be different?
+
Probably, but not because those things are wrong. Most approaches give you insight or tools without helping you understand which tool to use, when, and why. Reclaim Your Life gives you a structured map, starting with your specific survival state, so you're not guessing. The Somatic Pause app tells you what your body needs right now, not what helps in general. That specificity changes things.
I started something like this before and didn't finish it. What's different here?
+
The Milestone Tracker exists specifically for this. The journey is broken into 13 clear steps, you always know exactly where you are and what's next. There's no huge commitment required. You start with the quiz, which takes five minutes. Then one step at a time. The whole thing is designed to respect your capacity, not demand more of it.
How much time does this take?
+
As much or as little as you have. The quiz takes five minutes. The library content is designed to be consumed in short sessions, no long commitments. The Somatic Pause app is for whenever you need it, not a daily obligation. The whole structure is built around your pace, your body, your timeline.
What is the community actually like?
+
Honest. Women checking in, sharing what's working, asking for support when they're in it. It's not a place to perform being okay, it's a place to actually say where you are. People who are in the work, not performing it. That said, you can engage as much or as little as you want. Lurking is allowed. Showing up at your own pace is the whole point.
Is this therapy?
+
No, and it's not trying to be. This is education, community, and somatic practice tools. It doesn't replace clinical mental health support, and if you're in acute distress, please do seek professional care. What this offers is something different: a structured way to understand your nervous system patterns and work with them, not just cope around them.
Who leads this community?
